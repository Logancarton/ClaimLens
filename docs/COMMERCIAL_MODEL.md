# Commercial Model

## Commercial thesis

ClaimLens is valuable only if it reduces a cost or increases defensible revenue enough to justify its price.

## Initial value hypotheses

ClaimLens may create value by:

- Reducing preventable documentation/coding errors before submission.
- Reducing manual billing-review time.
- Identifying supported services that would otherwise be missed.
- Reducing unsupported claims and audit exposure.
- Reducing payer-specific rework/denials after payer overlays are added.

## Potential initial buyers

These remain hypotheses until validated:

- Individual psychiatric practices.
- Multi-provider behavioral-health groups.
- Billing/revenue-cycle companies.
- Larger outpatient clinics.
- Health systems after enterprise requirements are met.

## Possible delivery models

### Software
Per-provider, per-encounter, or organizational subscription.

### AI-assisted audit service
Customer provides an appropriately governed dataset/workflow; ClaimLens plus human validation produces an audit report. This may be a lower-friction path to early revenue and training/benchmark insight.

### Hybrid
Software performs continuous review while expert services support onboarding, rule configuration, or periodic audit.

## Commercial proof metrics

- Human minutes saved per reviewed encounter.
- Preventable error rate found before submission.
- Supported service capture missed by existing workflow.
- Reduction in denial/rework rate where measurable.
- Dollar value of corrected/prevented claim issues.
- False-positive burden imposed on staff.

## Current rule

Do not build enterprise features merely because a future hospital might want them. First prove that the core compiler creates measurable value in the narrow initial workflow.
